# Modelling a level

The geometry of a level: what to model, how to light it, how to mark water, lava and pits, and how to export. Studio
checks every file with the game's own track importer, so these rules are the game's.

## Set up the scene

- **Y is up**, as in Blender's glTF export (keep **+Y Up** on).
- **One Blender unit is one game unit.** For a sense of size: a step between platforms of 25 units is easy to climb, a
  door is about 144 high, the sample field is 2000 × 2000. Every position must stay within -32768…32767.
- **Collision is the drawn triangles.** Model the floor the player walks on as real faces. There's no separate
  collision mesh unless you make one (a `collider` surface).
- Faces are **counter-clockwise seen from their front** (Blender's normal direction). Turn on Backface Culling in the
  viewport to see which way faces point.

<Callout type="warning">
**Ramps and steps:** the hero can't climb a lip. Start ramps a little below the ground they rise from, and keep raised
floors (a bridge deck) reachable by a ramp rather than a step up. Juno walks under a deck she can't step onto and
falls.
</Callout>

## Organic ground

Boxes and flat floors make a level feel like a test room. jfg64 can make ground for you: run these in Blender's
Python console, or have an AI agent run them through a Blender MCP server.

```python
import jfg64
ground = jfg64.terrain("valley", size=(2400, 2400), height=110, seed=7, style="hills",
                       flat=[(0, -900, 220), (300, 700, 260)],             # clearings: x, y, radius
                       paths=[[(0, -900), (-250, -200), (150, 300), (300, 700)]],
                       rim=380)                                            # hills round the edge
print(jfg64.terrain_report(ground))   # how much of it is walkable, the steepest slope, triangles
for i, (x, y, z) in enumerate(jfg64.scatter_points(ground, 14, min_gap=220, max_slope=25)):
    jfg64.rock(f"rock{i}", radius=60, seed=i, location=(x, y, z))
```

<Figure src="/img/blender-terrain.png" alt="A green valley ringed by hills, with grey boulders scattered on its floor" caption="terrain() with a rim of hills, a path and two clearings, and rocks from scatter_points. Plain colours here; paint it with game textures or your own." />

- **`terrain`** builds the ground from layered noise:
  - `style`: `hills` for meadows, `ridges` for rocky mountains, `terraces` for steps;
  - `flat`: levelled clearings for the start, exits and squads;
  - `paths`: smoothed tracks between areas;
  - `rim`: a ring of hills that ends the level instead of walls.
  Slopes inside the rim are kept below the 45° the heroes can walk up. A new `seed` gives a new landscape.
- **`rock`** makes a lumpy boulder sunk into the ground. Rocks inside a squad's area become its cover in Studio.
- **`scatter_points`** finds spots on the gentle ground, a set distance apart, for rocks, pickups or props.
- Keep `step` (the grid size, default 50) between 40 and 80: every grid square is two triangles.

## Light it with vertex colours

The game's levels are lit by **vertex colours**: the baked light is the main look. Paint or bake into a colour
attribute (byte colour, face corner or point). It's exported as `COLOR_0` and multiplied with the texture: white is
"as the texture", darker is shadow. No vertex colours means full bright. The add-on's
[Bake vertex light](/blender#vertex-light) does this for you.

## Textures and materials

### The game's own textures

The **Game textures** box in the panel paints with the game's own level textures. They come from your own ROM:
JFG-Studio builds the library in its data folder the first time you open a project. Pick a texture by **Colour** or
by the **Game level** that uses it, then **Use on active object**.

A level painted with them carries none of them. Studio names each one by number, and each player's game shows it
from their own copy. That's how to make a forest level look like the game's forests. Two rules:

- **Don't edit their pixels.** An edited copy is a new image, and the game's art can't go in a pack: Studio's import
  check and build refuse it.
- They mix freely with images of your own.

### Your own images

- One image per material, as **PNG**. Small is right for an N64 game: 16×16 to 64×64. Studio cuts larger ones to
  sizes that fit the console's texture memory.
- UVs may repeat past 0…1 (tiling).
- **New images are small**: each becomes a new texture of the game's, stored as one 16-bit image of at most 2048
  pixels (64 × 32, 32 × 64…; bigger ones are shrunk). There's no small fixed number of them (the example world adds
  31), but reuse images across materials to keep the pack small (lava can reuse a stone image under red vertex
  colours). Studio's import check counts them.
- **Special surfaces come from words in the material name** (anywhere, separated by `_`, `-`, a space or a dot):

| Word | In the game |
|---|---|
| `water` | A water surface (see-through): the hero swims below its height. A flat face at the water's height, with alpha in the texture |
| `lava` or `damage` | A damage floor: pushes Vela and Lupus and hurts them. Juno passes through it, so model a solid floor about 30 below every lava surface, or it's a hole he falls through |
| `kill` | A kill plane: a pit floor that kills, below a drop |
| `collider` | Invisible but solid: walls that block without being drawn |
| `nocollide` | Drawn but not solid: grass tufts, decoration |
| `ledge` | The top of something the hero can grab the edges of: the walls just below its edges need nothing |

Examples: `rock_lava`, `pit_kill`, `glass_collider`, `pond_water`.

Advanced: the custom properties `batchFlags`, `pass` (`opaque` / `translucent`) and `triangleFlags` on a material set
the flags directly. Turn on **Custom Properties** in the export to keep them.

## Export

**File › Export › glTF 2.0** (or the add-on's **Export for JFG-Studio**):

- **Format**: glTF Binary (`.glb`).
- **Include**: Selected or Visible Objects (only the level), **Custom Properties** on.
- **Transform**: **+Y Up** on.
- **Mesh**: **Apply Modifiers** on, UVs on; vertex colours: the active one.
- **Materials**: Export; images Automatic (PNG).
- No animation, no armature: a level is static geometry.

## Checklist before Play

- The level has a floor under the start point (Studio places objects on the floor it finds).
- Pits have a `kill` floor, or the hero falls forever.
- As few new images as you can.
- Studio's import check has no errors, and **Validate** shows none either.

## When the check complains

| Message | Fix in Blender |
|---|---|
| A vertex out of range | Move the level closer to the origin, or make it smaller |
| A non-triangle primitive | Remove loose edges and points (export meshes only) |
| Two materials give one texture different images | Give each image its own material |
| Doesn't fit the track buffer | Fewer triangles, or split the level in two |
| More than 127 segments | Studio splits big meshes for you; a level this large needs splitting into two levels |

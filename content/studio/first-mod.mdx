# Your first planet

This walk-through makes a small pack from nothing: a level on your own geometry, a start point, an ant squad that
keeps a door shut, an exit home, a message when you arrive, and a planet on the map that leads to it. It takes about
half an hour. You need [Studio set up](/getting-started#installing-jfg-studio) with your ROM, the decomp and the port.

<Callout type="info">
Don't have a level model yet? Open the [example world](/studio/example-world) first and copy one of its `.glb`
files from `geometry/`, or follow [Modelling a level](/blender/modelling) to make a simple floor in Blender.
</Callout>

<Steps>
### Create the project

**File › New project…** opens the dialog:

<Figure src="/img/studio-new-project.png" alt="The New project dialog with Name, Mod id, Folder and Geometry fields" caption="Name, mod id, where to create it, and (optional) your first level's .glb." width={560} />

- **Name**: what players see in the Mods list, e.g. `My Planet`.
- **Mod id**: lower case and unique to you, e.g. `yourname.my_planet`. It names the pack and keeps your content apart
  from other people's, so don't reuse someone else's.
- **Folder**: where to make it. Studio creates `my_planet.jfgproj` inside.
- **Geometry**: your `.glb` from Blender. Studio copies it into the project, makes the first level on it, and puts
  start point 0 in the middle, on the floor. Markers from the [Blender add-on](/blender) become objects.

Press **Create**. The level opens in the Scene and the import check runs: its report is in the **Console**
(segments, triangles, new images, whether it fits the game's track buffer, and the special surfaces it found).

### Look around

Hold the right mouse button and use <kbd>W</kbd><kbd>A</kbd><kbd>S</kbd><kbd>D</kbd> to fly, <kbd>Q</kbd>/<kbd>E</kbd> to go
down and up; the mouse wheel changes the speed. <kbd>F</kbd> frames the selection, or the whole level from above.

<Figure src="/img/studio-scene.png" alt="The Scene: a green field with stone walls and ramps, markers and labels over the objects" caption="The Scene draws your geometry with its textures, vertex light and fog; objects are markers or the game's models." />

### Set up the level

Open the **Level** window (right side). Give the level a **Name** (31 characters at most), pick its **Music** (any
stock tune, or [your own OGG](/tutorials/music)), a **Sky** and **Fog**.

<Figure src="/img/studio-level.png" alt="The Level window: name, world, track, music and sound, sky and fog" caption="The Level window. Changes here can be undone like object edits." width={345} />

### Add an ant squad that locks a door

In **Prefabs** (bottom), open **Groups** and drag **Squad, region and locked door** into the Scene. Before you drop
it, pick the **Squadron** (an enemy type and its count), a **Door**, and how many **Members**.

<Figure src="/img/studio-prefabs.png" alt="The Prefabs window: search, In stock, Groups, Pack kinds and categories" caption="Prefabs: groups at the top, your pack's kinds, then every object type by category." />

You get a squadron, its members, a square **navigation region** they keep to, and a door that stays shut until
they're all dead. The Inspector shows each part's fields.

<Callout type="warning">
Every squadron needs a navigation region with at least three corners, or the game crashes when the level starts.
The group makes one for you; the validator catches a missing one.
</Callout>

For the ants to walk and come after the player, not just crouch and fire, their region needs cover: crates, blocks
or pillars inside it, made holes of the region. See [Squads that move](/studio/squads).

### Place pickups and move things

Search Prefabs for `health` and drag a few in. Select an object (click it, or in the **Hierarchy**) and:

- <kbd>W</kbd> moves it; with **Floor** on (toolbar) it drops onto the floor below. **Snap** moves in grid steps.
- <kbd>E</kbd> turns objects that have a facing; <kbd>R</kbd> sizes those that have a size.
- **Drop to floor** in the Inspector puts it on the floor, a little above it so it doesn't spawn inside a slope.

<Figure src="/img/studio-inspector.png" alt="The Inspector for a squadron: name, position, Drop to floor, and its fields" caption="The Inspector: the selected object's name, position and fields. Dimmed values come from the stock entry." width={345} />

### Add the exit home

Drag **Groups › Exit and return start point** near the edge of the level. Select the exit, and in the Inspector's
**Destination** pick **Leads to** (any of your levels or a stock level: the game's first level, stock level 21, is a good one) and
the **Start point** to arrive at.

### Say hello

Open **Triggers**, press **New trigger**, and set **When** to `level start`. Its first action is a `message`: type
your welcome. Press **Save triggers**.

<Figure src="/img/studio-triggers.png" alt="The Triggers window with two triggers, their level, condition and actions" caption="Triggers: when something happens in a level, then actions. No code needed." width={345} />

### Put it on the map

Open **Planet** and press **New planet**. Give it a **Name**, then click empty map space to place its disc and a
stock planet to draw the route from (only free stick directions are offered). Set **Entry level** for Juno, Vela and
Lupus to your level. **Opens** decides when it shows: `with the stock point its route leaves` is the simplest.

<Figure src="/img/studio-planet.png" alt="The Planet window: the map with planets and routes on the left, fields on the right" caption="The Planet window, here on a galaxy of the pack's own. See A galaxy of your own for that." />

### Validate, save and play

Press <kbd>Ctrl+S</kbd>, then **Validate**: the Console lists problems; click one to jump to the object. With no
errors, pick a hero next to **Play** and press it. Studio builds the pack, installs it into the port, and starts the
game in your level.

<Figure src="/img/studio-console.png" alt="The Console with the Validate button and 0 errors, 0 warnings" caption="The Console: validator results and the build and play output." />
</Steps>

## Sharing it

**Build › Build pack** writes `my_planet.jfgproj/build/<mod id>.nrm`. That file is your mod: players put it in their
`mods` folder. It carries only your content; the game's parts are filled in from each player's own ROM.

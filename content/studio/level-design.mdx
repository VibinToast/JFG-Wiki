# Level design

The game's levels aren't one big room. They are rooms of different sizes and shapes joined by corridors, tunnels and
paths, at a few different heights. This page gives the game's own proportions, measured from 274 of its levels, and
how to build levels that feel like them. AI agents read the same advice through Studio's `design_guide` tool.

## Outdoor or indoor

Few of the game's levels are half and half: most are mostly outdoors or mostly indoors, and the two are built
differently.

| | Outdoor | Indoor |
|---|---|---|
| Floor area | about 2,400 × 2,400 | about 1,600 × 1,600 |
| Height spread of the floor | about 300; climbing levels pass 1,000 | about 85: mostly flat |
| Passages (under 400 wide) | a tenth of the floor, about 250 wide | a third of the floor, about 200 wide |
| Big open ground (over 1,600 wide) | common | none |
| Ceiling | the sky | rooms 600–800 high, tunnels about 300 |

In both, floors rise gently: slopes, ramps and low steps.

### Outdoor levels

Typical shapes:
- open ground round a feature (a lake, a crater, a tower), with paths out to side areas;
- a basin whose paths climb round its sides;
- a canyon or riverbed winding between cliffs;
- islands reached by jetpack.

How to build one:
- **Edges**: end the level with hills, cliffs or water, not walls.
- **Areas**: put the start, fights, key and exit in clearings at different heights, joined by winding paths, with
  one loop.
- **Fill**: rocks, ruins and trees break up open ground.

### Indoor levels

Typical shapes:
- a main hall with side rooms, alcoves and doors;
- a round arena with a ring walkway round a pit;
- winding tunnels opening into small rooms;
- a chain of rooms;
- a cluster of small round chambers.

How to build one:
- **Rooms and corridors**: roof every room. Mix room sizes: one big hall and several smaller rooms, with a third of
  the floor in corridors and tunnels.
- **Cover**: pillars and crates in the rooms.
- **Light**: light rooms and doorways with lights, not the sun.

## Building it in Blender

The [jfg64 add-on](/blender) lays out rooms and passages for you:

```python
import jfg64
rooms = [{"name": "hall", "at": (0, 0), "size": (1100, 700), "shape": "hall", "roof": True},
         {"name": "cave", "at": (1700, 500), "radius": 450, "shape": "cave", "floor": 150, "roof": True},
         {"name": "yard", "at": (-300, 1700), "radius": 650, "floor": -120, "height": 500}]
links = [{"from": "hall", "to": "cave", "kind": "tunnel", "wiggle": 0.7, "via": [(900, 150)]},
         {"from": "hall", "to": "yard", "kind": "canyon", "width": 300, "via": [(-450, 800)]}]
level = jfg64.layout("level", rooms, links, floor=..., wall=..., ceiling=...)
print(jfg64.layout_report(level))
```

<Figure src="/img/blender-layout.png" alt="Five rooms of different shapes and heights joined by a winding tunnel, a corridor and canyons, roofs hidden" caption="layout(): rooms of their own shapes and floor heights, joined by tunnels, corridors and canyons that cut doorways in them. Roofs are hidden here." />

- **Rooms**: `round`, `cave` (rough) or `hall` (built). Each has its own `floor` height and a `roof` if it's indoors.
- **Links**:
  - `tunnel` is roofed, `corridor` has walls and open sky, `canyon` has tall walls.
  - They wind through `via` bends and `wiggle`, slope from one room's floor to the other's, and cut a doorway in
    each room.
- **Doors**: a door has to close its passage, and the game's doors overlap their frame. Give the link
  `"door": "from"` (or `"to"`, `"both"`): it builds a stretch of level floor with a 160 × 180 doorway, smaller
  than the game's 198 × 199 doors, and `jfg64.door_spots(level)` says where each door goes. A door in a room's
  doorway gets walked round; one as wide as its tunnel shows gaps round its edges. Studio's placement check says
  when a door doesn't close its opening or stands on a slope.
- **Defaults**: the game's own sizes (passages 200 wide, tunnels 300 high, rooms 600 high).
- **Climbs**: anything the player must reach needs steps of about 70. A key on a 210-high block was out of reach,
  which made the level unbeatable; Studio's placement check says when a key is out of reach.
- **Water** lies in a hollow, its edge meeting the bank. A water sheet raised over flat ground hovers.
- **Cover**: give each enemy its own. With fewer rocks than enemies, two ran to the same rock and overlapped.
- **Exits** are level changes: put them where the player expects to leave, such as the end of a passage. An exit
  back into its own level reloads it, enemies and all, and feels like a hidden teleport; Studio refuses one.
- **Outdoors**: [`terrain`](/blender/modelling#organic-ground) makes the open ground, rims and clearings.

## Building blocks

Beyond rooms and passages, the add-on has pieces for levels with their own shape:

- **`building(rooms, links)`**: square rooms (`"shape": "box"`) joined by straight corridors that turn at right
  angles, for bases, temples and ships.
- **A pit** in a room's floor (`"pit": {"radius": 330}`), down to a kill floor, and **`bridge`** to cross it.
- **`platform`** and **`ramp`**: balconies, stages, terraces and the slopes up to them (45° at most).
- **Raised doorways**: a passage can leave a room partway up its wall (`"from_floor": 180`), onto a balcony.

## Every level its own

Three trial levels built from the same blobs and tunnels felt the same to play. So each level gets:
- a **signature** set piece it's built round, such as a chasm crossing, a tower to climb, a corridor maze, a river,
  jetpack islands or an arena;
- a **room plan** unlike the world's other levels (hub, chain, loop, arena, terraces, islands, building, vertical);
- its own mix of room shapes, sizes and heights.

Agents may model anything else in Blender too, as long as it keeps to the game's limits and passes Studio's checks.

## Worlds of several levels

Each level ends at a door with an exit to the next level. A level can also end at two doors: one on along the
main path, the other into a side path of a level or two with Tribals to rescue, which rejoins the main path or
leaves the world.

## Comparing with the game

Studio's agent tools measure a level as the study did:
- **Your level**: `level_layout(level)` measures a level of the project.
- **The game's**: `stock_level_layout(level)` measures one of the game's levels, from your own copy of the game.

Each gives a floor map and the numbers to compare.

# Custom music

Your own music in your level, looping cleanly, and a different track when the boss gate opens.

<Steps>
### Make an OGG

Export your track as **OGG Vorbis** (Audacity: File › Export › Export as OGG). Stereo, 44.1 kHz is fine. Keep it
short-ish and loopable: Jet Force Gemini: Recompiled streams it.

### Set loop points (optional)

Without loop points the whole file loops. To loop a section, add two comments (tags) to the OGG, in samples:

| Tag | Value |
|---|---|
| `LOOPSTART` | The sample the loop goes back to (e.g. after an intro) |
| `LOOPLENGTH` | How many samples the loop lasts |

At 44.1 kHz, 2.5 seconds is 110250 samples. Audacity: **Edit Metadata** before export.

### Add it to the project

In the **Level** window, open **Music and sound** and press **Add OGG music…**. Studio copies it into `music/`.

<Figure src="/img/tutorial-music.webp" alt="The Level window's Music and sound section: a Music list, the Add OGG music button and three Ambient loop lists" caption="Music and sound in the Level window." width={344} />

### Pick it for the level

In the same section, choose it in **Music**. Your track plays in place of the game's tune and follows the game's music
volume, pauses and fades.

### Switch tracks from a trigger

In **Triggers**, make one with **When** `door opens` and the door's id, and add a `music` action with your second
track. Or from Lua: `jfg.music("victory")` and `jfg.music_stop()`.
</Steps>

## Stock tunes and ambience

The **Music** list also has every stock tune (listed with a level that uses it), and **Ambient loop 1–3** pick the
game's ambient sounds: wind, water, machinery. They cost nothing in your pack.

## By hand

Studio writes `jfg_music.json` in the pack:

```json
{ "tracks": { "boss": "music/boss.ogg" },
  "levels": { "level_arena": "boss" } }
```

A level can be one of yours (`level_x`) or a stock one (`stock:21`).
